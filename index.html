The Two-Track Buy Box

§01 · Parallel Acquisition Frameworks

DFW retail breaks into two fundamentally different investment archetypes. Track A — Suburban Premium trades on residential demographics, school clusters, and household income. Track B — Urban Infill trades on daytime population, walkability, district momentum, and tenant lifestyle mix. Both tracks are eligible for the platform; an asset only needs to win one.

Track A · The Suburban Strategy

Suburban Premium

"Where families with disposable income spend their evenings and weekends."

Geography

  • Counties: Collin, Denton, NE Tarrant — >50% pop. growth since 2010.
  • Top submarkets: Allen (3.4% vac), McKinney (+3.5% absorb), Frisco/W. Frisco, N. Collin Cty Outlying (+10.8% absorb #1), Northwest FW (+2.5% absorb #4), Irving (2.0% vac #6).
  • Hard exclude: SW Dallas County (8.2% vac, –1.7% absorb #68), W. Fort Worth (9.2% vac).

Demographics & Schools

  • Median HHI ≥ $100k in ZIP (DFW metro = $95k).
  • 5-yr pop growth ≥ 1.0%; income forecast ≥ 3.5%.
  • Multiple GreatSchools 8+ within 2 miles.
  • A/B-rated ISDs: Frisco, Allen, Plano, Keller, Lewisville, GCISD, Coppell, Northwest.

Asset & Deal Profile

  • Size: 7,000–30,000 SF · multi-tenant strip / neighborhood / grocery-anchored.
  • Total ask: ≤ $11M — first-acquisition equity check sizing.
  • Tenant mix: Daily-needs / service / F&B (fitness, medical, restaurant).
  • Vintage post-1995; CoStar Star Rating ≥ 3.
  • Cap 6.25–7.5%; price/SF below subtype avg ($284 Strip, $220 Nbhd).
Track B · The Urban Strategy

Urban Infill & Redevelopment

"Where daytime population, walkability, and district momentum drive the deal."

Target Districts

  • Fort Worth: Near Southside / Magnolia / Medical District, West 7th, Cultural District.
  • Dallas: Bishop Arts / N Oak Cliff (75208), Lakewood / Lower Greenville / Knox-Henderson (75204/75206), Deep Ellum, Trinity Groves.
  • Common thread: active redevelopment, walkable street grid, mixed-use density.

Population & Anchors

  • 3-mi population ≥ 100k (density over residential HHI).
  • Daytime employment anchor — medical district, university, employment hub.
  • Walkability ≥ 80; Walk-Score-style street grid.
  • Schools waived — F&B / lifestyle tenants don't recruit on school zones.

Asset & Deal Profile

  • Size: 7,000–30,000 SF · multi-tenant strip / street-front retail.
  • Total ask: ≤ $11M — first-acquisition equity check sizing.
  • Type: Restaurant / F&B / lifestyle / experiential, recently built or gut-renovated (post-2020 ideal); CoStar Star ≥ 3.
  • Cap 5.5–7.0% tolerated (urban infill premium); price/SF can exceed subtype avg if district rent growth supports.
  • WALT ≥ 4 years; watch tenant rollover concentration carefully.

Scoring Framework

§02 · Our Scoring Framework · Our Strategy + FrontView REIT · Two Scores, Blended 60 / 40

These twelve factors are what we settled on, over several iterations — pairing our own buy-box discipline with FrontView REIT's net-lease strategy — to read a submarket at a glance and flag what makes a durable retail hold. Each deal carries two scores — Real Estate and Tenant & Lease — blended 60 / 40. We weight it that way on purpose: in retail a strong tenant can't rescue weak real estate, so location, re-tenantability and demand do the heavy lifting. Move the weights in the Thesis panel below and every score, decision and map marker re-sorts live. Blended tiers: Premier ≥8.5 · Institutional 7.0–8.4 · Value-Add 5.5–6.9 · Deep Discount 4.0–5.4 · Pass <4.0. The hard gates below kill a deal regardless of how it scores.

Data Sources & Methodology

  • Our scoring framework — buy-box criteria combined with FrontView REIT's net-lease strategy: two parallel 1–10 scores — Real Estate (Traffic & Frontage 25% · Demographics & MSA 15% · E-Commerce Resistance 15% · Re-Tenantability 20% · Tenant Diversity 10% · Value Creation 15%) and Tenant & Lease (Credit 30% · Lease Structure 15% · WALT 20% · Escalators 15% · Renewal Options 5% · Rent Coverage 15%) — blended 60% RE / 40% Tenant. Weights are adjustable live in the Investment Thesis panel (§02.5); all scores, decisions, the ranking and the map recompute on change.
  • Hard gates (auto NO-GO): traffic < 10,000 VPD · trade-area population < 30,000 (10-mi) · specialized single-use building · WALT < 3 yr · non-IG asking cap > 50 bps below the FVR-implied fair cap.
  • Recommended cap band & fair value: cap (%) = MAX(5.5, 10.5 − blended × 0.525) ± 25 bps; implied fair value = NOI ÷ recommended cap. Benchmarked to FrontView REIT's Q1 2026 actuals — TTM acquisition cap 7.59%, disposition cap 6.79%, +80 bps spread.
  • Pipeline (39 deals): 19 with verified Offering Memoranda in the MO folder (click any GO/CAUTION deal to open its OM); 20 from CoStar / LoopNet exports (May 2026), flagged "No OM on file." Size / check-size filter: 7,000–30,000 SF · ≤ $11M ask.
  • Submarket fundamentals (rent, vacancy, 12-mo absorption, growth) from CoStar's Q2 2026 DFW Retail Market Report, covering 69 DFW submarkets / 470M SF — applied consistently per submarket. Demographics from OM radius reports where available, otherwise US Census ACS 2024 ZIP-level estimates.
  • Latest corrections (current build): Stacy Angel Crossing repriced to the verified OM ($5,708,000 / 6.25% / NOI $356,738; was $5,574,000 / 6.40%). North Collins Retail Center (1110 N Collins, M&M OM) separated from the distinct CoStar listing One Fifty-Seven Plaza (2104–2136 N Collins) — they are different buildings. Henderson Crossing construction year corrected to "undisclosed (remodeled 2026)." Priority cards and two-track lead picks are now generated live from the engine rather than hardcoded.
  • Data confidence: the four real-estate judgment factors (e-commerce resistance, re-tenantability, tenant diversity, value creation), plus traffic and 10-mi trade-area population where the OM is silent, are analyst estimates — strongest for the 19 OM-verified deals, directional for the 20 CoStar/LoopNet listings. This is a top-of-funnel screen, not a substitute for deal-level underwriting (DCF / IRR with debt, Texas tax reassessment, lease-by-lease rollover) and confirmatory diligence (T-12s, PCA, environmental, title, zoning, estoppels).

Important Caveats

  • McDermott — near-term rollover (7 months): Krossover Barber Shop (1,760 SF, lease expires Sep 30, 2026 — 4 months from today) and Moataz Pharmaceuticals / PillMart (1,500 SF, expires Dec 31, 2026 — 7 months from today) together represent 15.8% of GLA. Both lease renewals need to be negotiated during diligence period, not after close. Verify both tenants have given good-faith renewal indications before signing PSA. Build a downside model assuming one of the two vacates and the space takes 6–9 months to re-lease.
  • McDermott — tenant credit concentration: All 8 tenants are local/regional operators with no national corporate credit. Paradise Salon Suites (6,000 SF / 29.03% of GLA) is the largest single tenant; lease commenced April 2025 with only 13 months of operating history. Salon suite businesses can fail rapidly when sub-tenant occupancy drops. Verify Paradise's current sub-suite occupancy and revenue per suite, and request a personal guaranty from the operator before close.
  • McDermott — 28% mark-to-market thesis: Broker representation of "$30+/SF achievable market rent" needs independent verification. Pull comps for similar service-retail spaces on Allen's McDermott / Stacy / Watters corridors at the same vintage and SF range. If achievable market is actually $27–28/SF (not $30+), the mark-to-market upside drops from 28% to ~15%, materially affecting the value-add IRR.
  • McDermott — capex reserves on 20-year-old vintage: 2005–2006 buildings on 2.56 acres mean two roofs and likely 16–20 HVAC units at or past mid-life. The OM does not disclose system ages or recent capex. Request: roof warranty status (if any), HVAC unit ages and condition, last major capex spend, and any deferred maintenance. Build a reserve of $1.50–$2.00/SF/yr ($30–$40k/yr) into year-1 underwriting.
  • McDermott — Collin CAD reassessment: Current taxes $95,948 ($4.64/SF) per OM. Expect material step-up on close (typical Texas pattern). NNN structure passes the increase to tenants, but mid-lease CAM bill increases create renewal tension and may compress your achievable mark-to-market at 2026/27 expirations.
  • Henderson Crossing — tertiary submarket / Amazon catalyst: Cleburne (Johnson County) sits outside the Tier 1 Allen/Frisco/Plano demographic band — 3-mi avg HHI is $86,667 (vs. $137k at McDermott, $181k 1-mi at Stacy) and 3-mi population is only 33,918. The investment thesis depends materially on the Amazon 1.7M SF facility (1,000 jobs, summer 2026 opening) catalyzing demographic and rent growth in the Henderson Street corridor. Underwrite without giving full credit to Amazon — the deal needs to pencil on as-is demographics. If Amazon hires materialize, it's upside; if they slip, you're holding a tertiary asset at a tertiary basis-adjusted return.
  • Henderson Crossing — $537/SF, vintage undisclosed (remodeled 2026): Highest price-per-foot in the pipeline. Justified only if the 2026 remodel scope is real and the cap rate holds. Independently pull Johnson County sales comps before underwriting — DuWest OM provides no comp set. Typical Cleburne retail likely trades at $200–$350/SF; the $537 is a function of cap-rate purchase, not market basis.
  • Henderson Crossing — 2026 remodel scope verification: OM represents new roof with assignable 10-yr warranty, new utilities, and new plumbing. Roof warranty assignability is the most valuable single deliverable — verify it transfers cleanly to buyer and survives change-of-ownership. Request: contractor scope-of-work documents, permits pulled, inspection reports, roof warranty paperwork, and HVAC ages (not addressed in OM).
  • Henderson Crossing — re-tenanted center, not seasoned: 5 of 7 leases commenced in late 2025 or 2026 — seller did the leasing lift. No T-12 operating reality check that these tenants can pay these rents. Particularly watch Glow Nail Bar (3,206 SF / 22.1% GLA, personal guaranty only) and the three franchisee-operated tenants (Metro PCS, Jersey Mike's, Nothing Bundt Cakes) which lack corporate guarantees. Request 3–6 months of rent payment history from the seller.
  • Henderson Crossing — OpEx looks materially under-stated: OM projects $66,159 total OpEx ($4.55/SF) — half the norm for a neighborhood retail center. CAM of $1.04/SF is implausibly low; tax line of $2.13/SF is pre-reassessment-on-sale. Johnson CAD reassesses aggressively. NNN structure means tenants reimburse, but a 50–150% step-up in tax CAM bills is a real tenant relations / retention risk on these freshly-signed leases. Build a model with normalized $7–9/SF OpEx and run sensitivity on tenant retention at lease year 3.
  • Henderson Crossing — Chipotle rent concentration: Chipotle's $62.34/SF rent ($149,990/yr, corporate guaranty, lease through 2040) represents ~30% of base rent. Lower than Viridian's WellMed concentration but model the dark-rent scenario.
  • Viridian Marketplace — cap rate timing (severe): Marketed 6.50% is the YE June 30, 2027 in-place number. Day-1 economic cap (May 2026) is ~5.59% on $367,893 NOI — the Tobacco Retailer lease (1,427 SF, $51,372/yr) does not commence until August 1, 2026, and is currently unburned. Once Tobacco commences (Aug 2026), cap moves to 6.37%; full 6.50% requires both the Aug 2026 Tobacco commencement and the June 2026 WellMed escalation to land. 78 bps day-1 gap is 3× the gap on Stacy Angel. If Tobacco lease fails to commence or is delayed, the going-in yield slips materially. Negotiate seller credit covering the full forward-NOI delta through Year 1, plus a contingent escrow against Tobacco non-commencement.
  • Viridian — tenant concentration: WellMed is 69.1% of GLA and ~64% of base rent. While the credit is strong (Optum / UnitedHealth Group, the largest healthcare conglomerate in the US), this is functionally a single-tenant medical office deal with two pieces of incremental income. Lose WellMed at the 2031 expiration and you have a vacant building. Diligence WellMed's DFW network strategy, this clinic's patient volume, and renewal history at comparable WellMed locations across Texas.
  • Viridian — 2030–2031 rollover cliff: All three leases expire within an 18-month window: Avoca Coffee 12/31/2030, WellMed 7/31/2031, Tobacco Retailer 7/31/2031. WALT looks strong (~5.1 yrs) but the structure is a wall, not a staircase. This is appropriate for a 5-year yield play, not a long-term compounder. Plan exit, refi, or aggressive renewal posture beginning in early 2029.
  • Viridian — untested Tobacco tenant: 1,427 SF tobacco/vape retailer lease commences 8/1/2026 — three months from today. OM describes the operator as having 10 retail locations (5 c-stores + 5 vape/tobacco). Request full tenant financials, parent entity structure, personal guaranty status, and credit references before LOI. Tobacco/vape regulatory exposure (FDA, state-level vape bans) is a real category risk worth modeling.
  • Viridian — missing demographic disclosures: OM provides 1-mi avg HHI ($131,121) and 1-mi avg home value ($554,174) only — no median HHI at any radius, no 3-mi or 5-mi avg HHI. Student-adjacent catchments (UNT, 47,000 enrolled) typically skew average HHI upward of median. Pull independent ESRI or Esri Business Analyst 1/3/5-mi median and avg HHI before underwriting.
  • Viridian — NOI / rent roll reconciliation: Rent roll (p.8 of OM) totals $419,287 base rent. In-place NOI calc (p.9) shows $427,606 base rent. $8,319 delta reconciles via the mid-year June 2026 WellMed escalation being annualized in the NOI calc. Defensible accounting but model month-by-month at LOI rather than accepting the annualized number at face value.
  • Viridian — Denton CAD reassessment: Current taxes $85,984 ($6.42/SF) per Denton CAD 2025 grown 3% in OM. As with Stacy Angel, expect material reassessment on sale — Texas standard. Tenants are NNN so reimbursements flow through, but a step-up CAM bill creates tenant relations exposure mid-lease.
  • Stacy Angel Crossing — cap rate timing: 6.25% is on Year 1 forward NOI of $356,738 (effective May 1, 2027) at the verified OM price of $5,708,000. Day-1 economic cap on the current $342,659 NOI is 6.00%. Seller credits the monthly rent differential at closing. Confirm the credit calculation methodology and verify that every rent step in the rent roll is contractually committed (per the OM rent roll, scheduled increases are documented for NexGen, T3, Ethos, Peony, and Vivi — Best Brains has no scheduled bump in the rent roll table).
  • Stacy Angel Crossing — OM data quality: The Long Prairie Plaza sales comp on p.21 of the OM lists the same major tenants as the Frisco 5-Tenant Retail Center on p.20 — clearly a copy/paste error in the OM. Doesn't affect underwriting but flag for broker quality of work.
  • Stacy Angel Crossing — pricing context: $532/SF is at the top end of the OM's own sales comps (range $384–$591). Justified by 2020 vintage, 100% NNN occupancy, and Allen demographics, but limits exit flexibility if interest rates rise or cap rates expand at resale.
  • Per-asset diligence still required. This screen is a top-of-funnel filter; it does not replace lease-by-lease tenant analysis, capex inspection, environmental, title, or zoning review.
  • Web-sourced data may be incomplete. Cap rate, DOM, broker, and CoStar Star Rating fields for new properties were estimated where not disclosed in public LoopNet listings; verify all material economics directly with listing brokers.
  • Cap rates as listed may not reflect normalized NOI. Pro-forma cap rates can differ materially after re-tenanting, vacancy adjustments, and capex burn-off.
  • Submarket data is current to Q2 2026 and represents a snapshot; refresh upon LOI.
  • 5-yr cap-rate forecast trends to 6.7–6.9% across subtypes; assets buying at sub-6.0% caps face mark-to-market exposure on resale. Stacy Angel's day-1 6.00% leaves a thin cushion; Viridian's day-1 5.59% is below the long-run forecast band and creates real resale cap expansion risk if held beyond the 2030–2031 rollover window.
  • 314 South Main rollover risk: 78% of GLA (Atlas Bar 42% + Buena Vida 36%) rolls within 30 months. Structure due diligence around tenant renewal probability, replacement-cost rents, and re-trade leverage if either tenant signals departure.
